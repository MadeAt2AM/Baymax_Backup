# Cold Email Drafts — Tone Guide

Proven templates from 2026-08-17 session. The brief: "tell them I came across your listing on Google, didn't find a website, ask if they have one or are planning one." No salesy language, no scam vibes.

## The four-part structure that works

Every cold email follows this skeleton:

1. **Observation** (1-2 sentences): where you found them, what you noticed.
2. **Question** (1 sentence): the actual ask — the reason for the email.
3. **No-pitch disclaimer** (1-2 sentences): explicitly say this isn't a pitch.
4. **Soft close** (1 sentence): "one-line reply either way is appreciated."

Total length: ~120 words. Anything longer and you're pitching.

## Subject lines (pick one, don't combine)

- "Quick question about [Company]'s online presence"
- "Found your [Company] listing on Google"
- "Did [Company] used to have a website?"

Avoid: "Affordable website design!", "Grow your business online", "Special offer for [niche] in [area]"

## Personalization discipline

Personalize **one thing** per email:
- Company name in subject + opening
- Area/neighborhood you found them in
- Specific detail from their listing (e.g. "I see you're at Midview City")

Don't try to personalize the body — leads read carefully enough that anything not relevant feels fake.

## Sign-off

ALWAYS confirm sign-off name with user before sending. Default to first name + full name variants:

- "Christus Vincent" (Chris's preference 2026-08-17 — no brand mention)
- "Chris Vincent" (less formal variant)
- "Chris at MadeAt2Am" (only if user confirms brand is OK)

NEVER sign off with just a brand name. NEVER include a brand link unless user confirms.

## Tone anti-patterns (these trigger "scam" filter)

❌ "I'm reaching out to offer you a special opportunity..."
❌ "We help businesses like yours..."
❌ "I noticed your business is missing..."
❌ "Limited time offer..."
❌ "Let's schedule a call to discuss..."
❌ "Quick question" in subject + giant pitch in body

## Tone patterns (these read as genuine)

✅ "I was searching Google for [niche] in [area] and your business came up..."
✅ "I tried to find a website for you but couldn't track one down..."
✅ "Do you have a website at the moment? If yes, I'd love the link."
✅ "If not, is that something on your radar at all..."
✅ "No pitch in this email."
✅ "If you're open to it, I'd appreciate a one-line reply either way."

## Spacing between sends

- 15 seconds between sends at minimum
- Don't send more than ~20 emails per session from the same address in a short window
- If scaling beyond 20, use multiple sending accounts orpace over multiple days

## What to track per send

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Email Sent | "Yes" + date |
| Reply received | (leave blank until reply) |
| Reply sentiment | Positive / Neutral / Negative |
| Follow-up needed | Yes / No |
| Result | Booked call / Not interested / No reply / Wrong contact |

## Reference: full template (2026-08-17 aircon outreach)

```
Subject: Quick question about [Company]'s online presence

Hi [Company] team,

I was searching Google for aircon service companies in [Area]
and your business came up — I tried to find a website for you
but couldn't track one down, so I figured I'd write and ask.

Do you have a website at the moment? If yes, I'd love the link —
happy to be pointed in the right direction. If not, is that
something on your radar at all, or are you mostly getting work
through word of mouth and the phone?

No pitch in this email. I'm asking because I work with a handful
of Singapore service businesses on their online presence and
I'm trying to understand how the industry handles it — what's
working, what's not.

If you're open to it, I'd appreciate a one-line reply either way.

Cheers,
Christus Vincent
```

## Follow-up cadence (if no reply)

- Day 5: gentle nudge ("Just following up on my email last week...")
- Day 14: final attempt ("Last note from me on this...")
- After Day 14: move to archive, no more outreach from this channel

Don't send more than 3 emails to the same recipient within 30 days — that's harassment, not cold outreach.
