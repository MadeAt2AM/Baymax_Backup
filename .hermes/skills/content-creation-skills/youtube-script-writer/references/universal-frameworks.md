# Universal YouTube Script Frameworks

## Advanced Storytelling Models

### The Three-Act Structure (adapted for YouTube)

**Act 1 — Setup (Hook + Intro):**
Establish the problem, promise, or premise. Make clear who this is for and what's at stake. Front-load value — don't make viewers wait.

**Act 2 — Confrontation (Body):**
Explore the topic with rising complexity. Introduce challenges, misconceptions, or surprising angles. Build toward the key insight. This is where re-hooks and pattern interrupts live.

**Act 3 — Resolution (Climax + Conclusion):**
Deliver the payoff. Close every open loop. End on something memorable — a mindset shift, a challenge to the viewer, or a cliffhanger for the next video.

---

### The Emotional Roller Coaster Model

High-performing videos map emotional intensity like this:

```
OPEN: High energy (excitement / urgency / shock)
  ↓
BODY START: Slight dip (context, setup — keep it brief)
  ↓
MIDPOINT: Rising tension (the problem deepens, the stakes are real)
  ↓
CLIMAX: Peak energy (the "aha" moment, the big reveal)
  ↓
RESOLUTION: Calm but satisfying (recap, forward-looking CTA)
```

Never stay flat. Vary the emotional register every 2–3 minutes.

---

### The Hook-Event-Payoff Micro-Structure

Use this inside each body section to keep it feeling like a mini-video:

1. **Hook**: A question, claim, or tension that opens the section
2. **Event**: The story, explanation, or example that unpacks it
3. **Payoff**: The insight, lesson, or punchline that closes it — and teases the next section

## Pattern Interrupt Library

Use these transition phrases to reset viewer attention every 2–3 minutes:

**Tension builders:**
- "But here's the part nobody talks about..."
- "And then things get weird."
- "This is where most people completely screw it up."
- "I didn't expect this either."

**Stakes raisers:**
- "The number on that screen was not what I expected."
- "That mistake cost them six figures."
- "One line of code. That's all it took."

**Curiosity gaps:**
- "Before I show you the fix — you need to understand why this actually happens."
- "There's a reason nobody does it this way. And it's a bad reason."
- "The third point is the one that actually changes everything."

**Reframes:**
- "You've probably heard X. The truth is closer to Y."
- "Most tutorials skip this part. This is the part that matters."
- "I thought I understood this until I tried it."

## Retention Science (Key Facts)

- Largest viewer drop-off: **first 30 seconds** (YouTube analytics, consistently)
- Channels using structured scripts report **3.2x higher average view duration** (Creator Economy Report 2024)
- Retaining **70%+ of viewers past 30 seconds** dramatically increases algorithmic distribution
- Re-engagement moments at the **40–50% timestamp** reset viewer commitment and reduce mid-video drop-off
- Placing the **strongest value point within the first 90 seconds** is the highest-leverage retention move
- For YouTube Shorts: **payoff-first structure** (invert the usual order — value at 0–2 seconds)

## Open Loop Techniques

Open loops are unresolved promises that make viewers stay to get the answer.

**How to open a loop:**
- "There's one more thing I'm going to show you at the end that ties all of this together."
- "I'll come back to why that number matters in a second."
- "Keep that in mind — it becomes relevant when we get to part three."

**How to close a loop:**
- Return to the promise explicitly: "Remember when I said X? Here's why."
- Deliver more than expected: "I said I'd show you one fix. Here are three."
- Use the close as a setup for the CTA: "...and the next video goes even deeper on exactly that."

**Never open a loop you don't close.** Broken promises destroy trust and tank retention.

## Video Type Templates

### Tutorial / How-To
```
Hook: Show the end result first — "Here's what you're going to be able to do by the end."
Intro: Why most tutorials on this fail + why this one is different
Body: Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 (with "why" for each, not just "what")
Climax: The step that most people skip / the insight that makes it click
CTA: "Next video shows you how to take this further / troubleshoot X"
```

### Listicle (Top 5, 7 Mistakes, etc.)
```
Hook: Tease the most surprising item on the list (often #3 or the last one)
Intro: Why THIS list is different from every other one on this topic
Body: Each item = Hook + Example + Takeaway (never just a name and a description)
Climax: The final item should be the most counterintuitive or highest-value
CTA: "One more thing" tease or next video link
```

### Story / Case Study
```
Hook: Start at the most dramatic moment of the story
Intro: Brief context — who, what, why it matters
Body: Chronological story arc with rising stakes
Climax: The turning point / the lesson revealed
CTA: "Here's what this means for you" or related deep-dive
```

### Explainer / Concept Deep-Dive
```
Hook: Common misconception about the topic
Intro: "Here's what's actually going on" — set up the reframe
Body: Layer complexity progressively — simple → nuanced → expert
Climax: The insight that reframes everything they thought they knew
CTA: Practical application video or follow-up
```

## Common Script Mistakes to Avoid

1. **The slow open**: Any version of "Welcome back to the channel, today we're going to..."
2. **Front-loading credentials**: Don't establish expertise before establishing value — do both simultaneously
3. **The feature dump**: Listing facts without WHY they matter to the viewer
4. **Flat pacing**: Same energy, same sentence length, no variation for 10 minutes
5. **The drag-out outro**: Talking for 90 seconds after the main content ends
6. **Broken loops**: Promising to reveal something and then forgetting to
7. **Double CTA**: "Like, subscribe, and also check out this video and leave a comment below" — pick ONE
8. **Passive language**: "It can be seen that..." → "Here's what's happening."
9. **Over-explaining the obvious**: Trust your audience — if they're watching a video on X, they know the basics
10. **Missing the re-hooks**: Letting the middle 40% of the video go flat
