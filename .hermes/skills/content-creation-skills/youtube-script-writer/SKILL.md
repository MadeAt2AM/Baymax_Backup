# YouTube Script Writer

A skill for crafting high-retention, human-sounding, viral-optimized YouTube scripts.

## How to Use This Skill

1. **Identify what the user needs** (see Workflow below)
2. **Identify the niche** — general, cybersecurity/hacking, or tech/AI/vibe coding
3. **Read the relevant reference file** for niche-specific guidance:
   - All niches → `references/universal-frameworks.md`
   - Cybersecurity/ethical hacking → `references/niche-cybersecurity.md`
   - Tech/AI/vibe coding/building-in-public → `references/niche-tech-ai.md`
4. **Follow the workflow** for the requested output type
5. **Apply the tone rules** — always conversational, never robotic

---

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Gather Context (if missing)

Before writing, confirm:
- **Video title or topic** (required)
- **Target audience** (beginner, intermediate, advanced; who they are)
- **Video length goal** (short <8 min, medium 8–15 min, long 15+ min)
- **Channel niche** (general, cybersecurity, tech/AI, other)
- **Tone preference** (casual/fun, authoritative, story-driven)
- **One-prompt or skeleton-first** (ask if not obvious)

If the user gives enough context, proceed directly. Don't ask unnecessary clarifying questions.

### Step 2 — Choose Output Mode

| User Request | Output Mode |
|---|---|
| "Write me a full script" | Full Script (Hook → Intro → Body → CTA) |
| "Give me an outline / skeleton" | Skeleton Structure |
| "Write just the hook / intro" | Hook or Intro Only |
| "Write section by section" | Point-by-point Body |
| "Two-click / one prompt" | Full Script |

## The Core Script Formula

Every YouTube script follows this structure. Never deviate from it:

```
HOOK (0–15 sec)
  ↓
INTRO / VALUE CONTRACT (15–60 sec)
  ↓
BODY — broken into 3–5 sections, each with:
  • What (introduce the point)
  • Why (why it matters to the viewer)
  • How (proof, example, steps, story)
  • Re-hook / pattern interrupt (every 2–3 min)
  ↓
CLIMAX / KEY MOMENT (the "aha" payoff)
  ↓
CONCLUSION / RECAP (brief — don't drag it)
  ↓
CTA (max 1–2 lines)
```

---

## The Hook — Most Critical Part

The hook determines whether someone watches or scrolls. Biggest viewer drop-off: first 30 seconds. Goal: retain 70%+ past 30 seconds.

### 3 Hook Types (pick one per video):

**#1 — The Direct Hook**
Calls out a specific person with a specific problem.
- ❌ "Are you someone who wants to be more secure online?"
- ✅ "If you're a developer who thinks their app is safe — this video is going to ruin your day."

**#2 — The Controversy Hook**
Says something that provokes a reaction, then backs it up.
- ❌ "Here's why cybersecurity is important."
- ✅ "The most dangerous hacker in the room is usually the IT guy you hired to protect you."

**#3 — The Negative Hook**
Leads with what NOT to do. Humans are wired toward loss-aversion.
- ❌ "Here's how to build your first AI app."
- ✅ "Stop building AI apps this way. Seriously, stop."

### Hook Rules:
- Max 2 lines / 3–15 seconds of spoken content
- NEVER start with "Welcome back", "Hey guys", "My name is", or "Today we're going to talk about"
- Must validate the thumbnail/title within the first 2 seconds
- Open an information gap — make them feel they NEED to keep watching
- End the hook on an incomplete thought or tension

---

## Intro / Value Contract (15–60 sec)

Uses the **Hook → Shock → Validate → Tease** framework:

- **Hook**: Already delivered — carry the energy forward
- **Shock**: Drop a surprising stat, fact, or claim that raises the stakes
- **Validate**: Tell them WHY they should trust this video / why it matters to them specifically
- **Tease**: Preview what's coming WITHOUT giving it all away — open a loop

### Example (Cybersecurity):
> "Last year, over 40,000 companies got hit by SQL injection — a vulnerability that's literally been known since 1998. That's not a new problem. That's a lazy problem. And in this video, I'm going to show you exactly how attackers do it, why most developers still miss it, and the three-line fix that would have saved all of them."

### Example (Tech/AI/Vibe Coding):
> "I shipped a full SaaS product last week. No co-founder, no dev team, no funding. Just me, Claude Code, and three days. And I'm going to walk you through every single decision — the stuff that worked, the stuff that completely broke, and the one AI tool that made the whole thing possible."

## Body — Retention Engineering

### The What / Why / How structure (per section):
Each body section should answer these three questions in order. Don't skip any.

### Re-hooks (Pattern Interrupts)
Insert every 2–3 minutes of content. These are sentences that:
- Create a new tension: "But here's where it gets weird..."
- Tease what's next: "And the third one — this one actually shocked me."
- Drop a surprising stat mid-point: "By the way — 94% of phishing attacks start with exactly what I just showed you."
- Use contrast: "Most people do X. But what actually works is..."

### Storytelling Beats
Use the narrative arc even in educational content:
1. **Setup** — the problem / challenge / situation
2. **Conflict** — why it's harder than expected, what goes wrong
3. **Resolution** — the insight, the fix, the win

### Pacing
- Short punchy sentences for momentum
- Vary sentence length — long sentence to build context, then short punch
- 130–160 words per minute is the sweet spot when spoken
- No paragraph longer than 4–5 sentences before a break or transition

---

## Conclusion & CTA

### Conclusion:
- Recap the 3 biggest takeaways in 2–3 sentences max
- Close the loop opened in the intro (deliver the promise)
- End on a forward-looking or memorable line — not "so yeah, that's it"

### CTA Rules:
- Max 1–2 lines
- Either: watch the next video OR subscribe — never both
- Make it feel natural, not obligatory
- Best CTA placement: right after the most valuable moment in the video, before the outro drags

### CTA Examples:
- "If you want to see how I actually hacked this in a live lab — that video's right here."
- "I built a whole series on this — the next part drops exactly where you need it most."
- "If this helped, the subscribe button is right there. Only if you want more of this."

---

## Tone & Language Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These apply to every script regardless of niche:

1. **Write like you talk** — contractions, natural pauses, real sentences
2. **Simple vocabulary** — if a 12-year-old wouldn't know the word, replace it
3. **No filler phrases**: "essentially", "at the end of the day", "it's worth noting", "embarking", "delve into", "folks", "enchanting", "groundbreaking"
4. **No stage directions** unless the user asks
5. **No fake enthusiasm**: "That's SO cool!", "Amazing!", "Wow!"
6. **Humor is fine** — dry wit, irony, self-aware asides work well
7. **Idioms and metaphors** welcome — but one at a time, don't mix them
8. **Never lecture** — talk TO the viewer, not AT them
9. **Don't pad** — every sentence must earn its place

## Skeleton Structure Mode

When the user wants an outline first, produce this for each section:

```
## [Section Name]

**What to include:** [content elements]
**What the viewer expects:** [information + how it should land]
**How to present it:** [flow, format, storytelling approach]
**Reference/data to include:** [stats, studies, examples to research]
**Example lines:** [1–3 sample sentences in the channel's voice]
```

| Video Length | Word Count Target |
|---|---|
| 5–8 min | 750–1,200 words |
| 8–12 min | 1,200–1,800 words |
| 12–20 min | 1,800–3,000 words |
| 20+ min | 3,000+ words |

Base rate: ~150 words/minute spoken.

## Output Format

Default output: **Markdown with H2/H3 section headings** for the script sections.  
Do NOT include labels like `[HOOK]`, `[CTA]` in the final script unless user asks — just write the script naturally, using headings only at major body sections.

For skeleton/outline: include the full structured breakdown per section.
