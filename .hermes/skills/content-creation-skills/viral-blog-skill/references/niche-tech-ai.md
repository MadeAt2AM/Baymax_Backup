# Niche Reference: Tech / AI / Vibe Coding / Building in Public

## Who Reads This Content

**The Vibe Coder / Non-Technical Builder**
Uses Cursor, Replit, Lovable, v0, Claude to build products without being a "real" developer. Wants honest walkthroughs, tool comparisons, what actually works vs. what breaks. Shares content that validates their approach or saves them from a mistake they were about to make.

**The AI-Curious Developer**
Traditional developer integrating AI into their workflow. Wants depth — not hype. Suspicious of "AI will replace developers" narratives. Reads DEV.to, Hacker News, The Pragmatic Engineer. Shares content that cuts through the noise.

**The Indie Hacker / Solo Founder**
Building in public. Cares about MRR, growth, distribution, ship velocity. Reads Indie Hackers, Twitter/X, Product Hunt. Shares milestones, lessons from failures, "how I built X in N days" posts.

**The AI Enthusiast / Tech Follower**
Not necessarily building. Follows the space. Wants to understand what's happening with LLMs, new model releases, AI products. Reads The Decoder, The Rundown AI, TLDR. Shares things that make them sound like they're in the know.

**The Tech Leader / Engineering Manager**
Evaluating AI tools for their team. Wants ROI framing, realistic capability assessments, adoption playbooks. Reads InfoQ, Pragmatic Engineer, HBR Tech. Shares things that help their team.

## The Content Tone Guide for This Niche

**What works:**
- Radical transparency: real numbers, real timelines, real struggles
- "Here's what I actually built" > "Here's what you could theoretically build"
- Candid about failures — the community respects honesty over polish
- Strong opinions, loosely held — take a stance, defend it, update when wrong
- Show the work: screenshots, code snippets, dashboards, before/after
- Self-aware humour about the absurdity of the space
- Builder voice: "I shipped this," "here's what I learned," "week 4 update"

**What kills credibility instantly:**
- Hype without substance ("AI is going to change everything!")
- Claims without proof ("This tool 10x'd my productivity")
- Fake failure posts that are actually humble brags
- Jargon stacking without explanation
- "Vibe coding" success stories that skip over the 6 hours of debugging
- Obsessing over tools, neglecting the problem being solved

## High-Performing Content Formats

### 1. The Build Log / Ship Update (Building in Public)
The most native format for this community. Raw, real, timestamped.

Anatomy: What I set out to do → What I actually did → What broke → What I learned → What's next + metrics

Example titles:
- "Week 12 of Building in Public: $847 MRR, 2 Churn Explanations, and Why I Almost Quit"
- "I Built a SaaS in 72 Hours Using Only AI Tools — Here's the Honest Breakdown"
- "Day 30: What I Shipped, What I Broke, and Why That's the Best Outcome"

Virality trigger: **Validated** (for people on the same path) + **Smart** (specific lessons that transfer).

Posting strategy: Cross-post the update as a Twitter/X thread first (the format originated there), then expand into a full blog post.

### 2. The AI Tool Battle Test
The community is flooded with tool launches. What they actually want is: "Does this work? Compared to what I'm already using?"

Anatomy: The claim made by the tool → My actual test setup → What worked → What didn't → Verdict vs. alternative → Who should use it

Example titles:
- "I Tested Every AI Coding Assistant for 30 Days. Here's What Actually Shipped Features."
- "Cursor vs. Claude vs. Copilot: Which One Actually Makes Me Faster? (With Real Data)"
- "The Vibe Coding Stack That Actually Works in 2026 (After Trying 12 Tools)"

GEO note: Tool comparisons with definite verdicts ("Cursor wins for X, Copilot wins for Y") are highly citeable. AI engines love a clear answer to "which is better?"

AEO note: Add a comparison table and a "Quick Answer" box at the top — this gets featured snippet placement when people search "[Tool A] vs [Tool B]."

### 3. The "How I Actually Built X" Walkthrough
Not a theoretical tutorial — a real post-build retrospective.

Anatomy: The idea → The stack chosen (and why) → Step-by-step what I did → The specific moments where it nearly broke → The final result (with metrics/screenshot) → What I'd do differently

Example titles:
- "How I Built a $0/month AI Newsletter Tool Using Claude API + Zapier + Beehiiv"
- "From Idea to First Paying Customer in 11 Days: The Actual Tech Stack and Workflow"
- "I Vibe-Coded a Chrome Extension. Here's Why the First 3 Approaches Failed."

Virality trigger: **Smart** (they get the shortcut) + **Inspired** (if they didn't build it yet).

SEO note: Include the specific tool names, model versions, and tech stack in the headline and body — these are long-tail search goldmines.

### 4. The Honest AI Capabilities Post
The niche is oversaturated with hype. Honest, nuanced capability assessments are rare and therefore highly shareable.

Anatomy: The claim/hype → What the model/tool can actually do → Where it breaks down → Real use case where it shines → Real use case where it disappoints → The practical verdict

Example titles:
- "What Claude 4 Is Actually Good At (And Where It Still Falls Apart)"
- "AI Agents in 2026: What They Can Do, What They Can't, and What's Pure Marketing"
- "The Honest Assessment of Vibe Coding: Who It's For and Who It Will Frustrate"

Community note: The AI-curious developer is deeply sceptical of hype. Posts that validate their scepticism while giving them accurate capability maps get massive engagement on Hacker News and DEV.to.

### 5. The Framework / Mental Model Post
The community loves abstraction that makes sense of chaos. A new, named framework for thinking about an AI/coding/building problem — if it's genuinely useful — spreads fast.

Anatomy: The problem people are having → Why existing mental models don't fit → The new framework (named, visualisable) → How to apply it → 3 examples of it in action

Example titles:
- "The Senior Architect Mindset for Vibe Coding: Why AI is Your Junior Dev, Not Your CTO"
- "Stop Building Features. Start Building Loops. (A Mental Model for AI Products)"
- "The Three Layers of an AI Workflow: Why Most People Only Build the First One"

GEO note: Named frameworks are extremely citeable. If your framework name is distinctive and the concept is useful, AI engines will reference it when answering related questions.

## Building in Public — Specific Content Strategy

Building in public (BIP) is both a distribution strategy AND a content format. The goal is to make your journey the content while also building an audience for whatever you're making.

**The BIP Content Calendar:**
- **Weekly**: Ship update — what you built, what broke, metrics (even if embarrassing)
- **Monthly**: Retrospective — what you learned, what you'd change, where you're heading
- **Milestone posts**: $1 MRR, first customer, first churn, $1K MRR, product hunt launch
- **Failure posts**: These outperform everything. Honesty about failure builds more trust than success posts.

**BIP tone rules:**
- Use "I" not "we" unless you have co-founders (and even then, be specific about who did what)
- Include actual numbers. "$800 MRR" beats "growing revenue." "3 paying customers" beats "early traction."
- Show the dashboard screenshot. Show the Stripe notification. Authenticity is the product.
- Admit when you pivoted, when you almost quit, when you were wrong

**The BIP virality formula:**
Specific struggle + Unexpected insight + Actionable lesson + Real metric = Highly shareable post

Example:
> "I spent 3 weeks building a feature my users asked for. After launching, exactly 0 of them used it. Here's what I learned about the difference between what users say they want and what they actually do."

This performs because it triggers **Validated** (every builder has had this experience) and **Smart** (the lesson is transferable).

## Vibe Coding Content — Specific Notes

"Vibe coding" (describing your app in plain English and letting AI handle the implementation) is a major content topic right now, but the space is polarised:

**Camp A — Utopians**: "AI can build anything! No-code future!"
**Camp B — Sceptics**: "Vibe coding produces unmaintainable spaghetti that breaks at scale."

The content that cuts through takes the nuanced third position:
- **"Vibe coding works for X, breaks for Y — here's the honest map"**
- Specific about what kind of projects vibe coding is suited for (MVPs, prototypes, internal tools, scripts) vs. where it falls apart (production systems, security-critical code, complex state management)
- Personal experience + code/output examples as evidence

**Vibe coding content that goes viral:**
- "I built X using only prompts in Cursor. Here's the actual prompt history." (Show the prompts — this is rare and extremely valuable to readers)
- "My vibe-coded app hit 100 users. Then this happened." (Scale-moment failures are deeply relatable)
- "The 5 prompting strategies that actually make AI write production-ready code"

**The "Senior Architect" framing** (coined in the DEV.to community):
The effective vibe coders treat AI as a junior developer. They define the architecture, set constraints, review output, and manage the AI — not blindly paste whatever comes out. Content explaining this mental model performs extremely well.

## Content Structure Template: The Build Retrospective

```
HEADLINE: 
  "I Built [X] Using [Tools] in [Timeframe] — Here's Exactly What Happened"
  OR: "Week [N] Building in Public: [Specific Metric], [Honest Observation]"

HOOK (2–3 sentences):
  → Start with the most interesting/surprising/counterintuitive moment from the build
  → Example: "On day 6, the AI confidently generated code that deleted my entire 
     test database. I watched it happen in real time and learned more in that 
     moment than in the previous 5 days combined."

QUICK CONTEXT (for AEO / new readers):
  → "What I'm building: [X]. Why: [Y]. Current status: [Z]."
  → 2–3 sentences max. Skim-friendly.

BODY:

  H2: What I Set Out to Build
    → The original idea. The expected timeline. The tools I planned to use.
  
  H2: What Actually Happened (The Honest Version)
    → The deviations. The breakages. The moments of "oh no."
    → This section is where virality lives — don't sanitise it.
  
  H2: The Stack / Workflow That Worked
    → Specific tools, model versions, prompting strategies
    → Include actual prompts where relevant — the community loves this
    → Screenshots, code snippets, architecture diagrams if helpful
  
  H2: What Broke and Why
    → The specific failure points
    → What you learned from each
    → "I would have avoided this by..."
  
  H2: The Metrics (If You Have Them)
    → Users, revenue, time spent, features shipped
    → Even "0 users and $0 revenue after 2 weeks" is useful data — include it
  
  H2: What I'm Doing Next
    → Creates a reason to follow/subscribe
    → Honest about uncertainty: "I don't know if X will work, but I'm testing it"

FAQ SECTION:
  → Questions your readers will have after reading
  → Often: "What tools did you use?", "How long did X take?", 
    "Would you recommend this approach?", "What would you do differently?"

CLOSE:
  → One clear CTA: Follow for weekly updates / Subscribe to newsletter / 
    Reply with your experience
  → Link to previous update if this is a series
```

## Platform Strategy for This Niche

**Primary publishing platforms:**
- **Hashnode**: Best for owned audience + developer community. You own the email list.
- **DEV.to**: Organic discovery, developer-friendly, good for technical tutorials
- **Your own site (WordPress/Ghost)**: For long-term SEO authority

**Avoid**: Purely publishing on Medium (no audience ownership post-2025 policy changes).

**Distribution channels:**
- Twitter/X: Native home of building in public. Post the key insight as a standalone tweet + thread. Link to full post.
- LinkedIn: For the business/product framing. Reframe the post as "lessons for builders."
- Hacker News (Show HN): For technical posts. "Show HN: I built [X] using [Y]" — be ready to engage in comments.
- Indie Hackers: Product milestone posts and retrospectives
- r/SideProject, r/startups, r/MachineLearning, r/LocalLLaMA (for AI-specific content)
- Product Hunt: For actual product launches, not blog posts

**Newsletter syndication:**
- TLDR AI / TLDR Tech
- The Rundown AI
- Import AI
- Ben's Bites
- Lenny's Newsletter (for product/growth angle)

Pitch these with a one-paragraph summary and your post link. A citation in TLDR AI alone can drive 500–2,000 readers and significant domain authority.

## The Building in Public Emotional Arc

The most viral building in public posts follow one of these emotional arcs:

**The Phoenix Arc**: "I nearly quit → I pushed through → Here's what changed"
(Triggers: Inspired + Validated)

**The Humbling Arc**: "I thought I had it figured out → I was wrong → Here's the real lesson"
(Triggers: Validated + Smart)

**The Discovery Arc**: "I stumbled into something that works and I'm not sure why → Here's what I found"
(Triggers: Smart + Curious)

**The Counter-Narrative Arc**: "Everyone says [X] about building in public. My experience was the opposite."
(Triggers: Indignant + Smart)

Engineer your posts around one of these arcs. Without an arc, it's a report. With an arc, it's a story people share.
