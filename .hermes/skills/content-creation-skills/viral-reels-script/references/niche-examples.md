## 9. CYBERSECURITY / ETHICAL HACKING

This niche has a highly engaged, skeptical audience. They respond to technical credibility, real demonstrations, and "things your IT department won't say out loud." Myth-busting and fear-to-empowerment arcs perform extremely well. Keep language precise — this audience will call out vagueness instantly.

**Best hook styles:** Curiosity Gap, Contrarian, High Stakes, Scroll-Stop Command
**Best CTA types:** Save, Comment (for DM automation), Follow for series
**Visual tips:** Terminal windows, Kali Linux UI, Wireshark captures, phone/laptop screen recordings, blur sensitive data, dark aesthetic works well

### Cybersecurity Niche Notes

- **Credibility is everything** in this niche. Show real tools (Kali Linux, Burp Suite, Wireshark, nmap) even briefly — it signals expertise instantly.
- Always clarify ethical/legal context. Add "in a legal lab environment" or "this is for educational purposes" where demos are shown. Avoids platform flags and builds trust.
- **Fear → Empowerment arc** performs best: open with a scary real-world scenario, close with actionable defense or skill-building.
- Avoid overhyping "I hacked X" — the audience is technical enough to be skeptical. Specificity and precision beat sensationalism.
- **Series format works extremely well** here: "Week 1 of learning ethical hacking" builds a loyal returning audience.
- Use dark/terminal aesthetic in overlays — it matches the community's visual identity.

**Best hook styles:** Transformation Proof, Steal/Direct Value, Curiosity Gap, POV

**Visual tips:** Screen recordings of builds, AI tool interfaces (Cursor, Claude, v0, Replit), revenue dashboards, Notion/Linear setups, lofi desk shots, terminal output, before/after of a product

### Tech / AI / Vibe Coding / Building in Public Niche Notes

- **Building in public** content thrives on raw numbers and real emotions. MRR, user counts, failure rates — specificity beats vagueness every time. "$0 revenue, week 3" outperforms "early days of my startup."
- **Vibe coding content** (using AI to build without traditional coding skills) is a massive trend. The key angle is accessibility: "you don't need to be a developer" is the emotional core.
- **Two audiences, one video**: write so a technical founder nods along AND a curious non-technical viewer understands the stakes. Avoid jargon without explanation.
- **Tool demos > talking heads** in this niche. Even 3 seconds of a real interface (Cursor, Claude, Replit, v0) signals credibility and stops scrolling.
- **Series format is king** for building in public. Weekly update Reels compound over time — each episode builds on the last, creating a returning loyal audience and a narrative arc viewers feel invested in.
- **Controversy works carefully**: "AI is replacing [job]" hooks get massive reach but must be followed by empowerment, not doom — otherwise you lose the audience in the comments.
- **Authenticity is the differentiator**: polished AI content is everywhere. Raw screen recordings, real errors, honest failures, and unfiltered reactions are what go viral in this space in 2026.

## Notes on Niche Adaptation

- **Fitness/Food**: Lead with visuals. Hook works alongside what's on screen.
- **Business/Finance**: Authority hooks work best. Numbers and specificity build trust.
- **Beauty/Skincare**: Science-backed hooks perform well ("Studies show…", "Dermatologists say…")
- **Relationships**: POV and identity hooks drive the most shares and saves.
- **Education**: Contrarian and scroll-stop hooks work best (challenge what they believe).
- **Content Creation**: Meta-hooks (hooks about hooks) perform exceptionally in this niche.
- **Cybersecurity**: Fear → Empowerment arc. Technical credibility signals (real tools, precise language) are non-negotiable. Ethical/legal disclaimers build trust, not fear.
- **Tech/AI/Vibe Coding**: Raw authenticity beats polish. Real numbers, real tools, real failures. Serve both builders and curious observers simultaneously.
